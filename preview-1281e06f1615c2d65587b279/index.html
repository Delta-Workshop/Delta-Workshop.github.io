3rd Workshop on Deep Generative Models in Machine Learning:
Theory, Principles and Efficacy

ICLR 2027, April 29–30, San Francisco, USA

Call for Papers

Description

Deep generative models support image and audio synthesis, language generation, and scientific modeling. Their usefulness depends on how they learn a distribution and how that learned distribution changes during sampling, acceleration, and adaptation.

The third DeLTa workshop brings together researchers in learning theory, probability, dynamical systems, statistical physics, and applied generative modeling. Building on the 2025 and 2026 editions, we aim to connect mathematical understanding with practical decisions in generative modeling.

Scientific Scope

  • Learning structure: learning dynamics, data geometry, representation learning, memorization, generalization, and statistical physics of generation.
  • Efficient sampling: diffusion and flow matching, numerical methods, stochastic dynamics, few-step generation, and distillation.
  • Reliable control: guidance, constraints, post-training, robustness, and evaluation in scientific and multimodal applications.

The scope spans continuous and discrete generative models, with connections to scientific discovery, audio, biology, and trustworthy AI.

Call for Papers

Preliminary CFP. Submissions are not yet open; the OpenReview portal will be announced here.

We invite submissions to the 3rd DeLTa Workshop on Deep Generative Models in Machine Learning: Theory, Principles and Efficacy. We welcome theoretical, methodological, and empirical research that advances our understanding of how generative models learn, sample, and adapt, and how these insights can improve their efficiency and reliability.

We encourage new and ongoing work, focused theoretical results, carefully designed experiments, and perspectives that identify important open problems. Contributions from early-career researchers and researchers across disciplines are especially welcome.

Topics of Interest

  • Theoretical foundations and learning dynamics: optimization, convergence, generalization, memorization, implicit bias, and feature learning in diffusion, flow-matching, autoregressive, and energy-based models.
  • Geometry, probability, and statistical physics: score estimation, data and latent geometry, manifold learning, optimal transport, Schrödinger bridges, and stochastic control.
  • Efficient and accurate sampling: numerical analysis of ODE/SDE solvers, discretization error, MCMC, distillation, consistency models, and one-step or few-step generation.
  • Discrete and structured generation: diffusion language models, generation on graphs and manifolds, token ordering, parallel decoding, and compositionality.
  • Post-training, adaptation, and control: guidance, conditioning, preference optimization, reinforcement learning, robustness, uncertainty, and evaluation.
  • Multimodal generative modeling: image, audio, video, and language generation, including connections between theoretical principles and scalable systems.
  • Generative models for science: physics, biology, molecular and protein design, chemistry, and materials, including inverse problems, physical constraints, and reliable scientific sampling.

Submission Format and Review

  • Short/Tiny Papers: up to 4 pages, excluding references and appendices. We welcome focused results, simple ideas, informative experiments, and work in progress.
  • Long Papers: up to 8 pages, excluding references and appendices.
  • Submit an anonymized PDF using the ICLR 2027 style files. Reviewing will be double-blind; supplementary material should also preserve anonymity.
  • Submissions will be assessed for technical soundness, clarity, relevance, and the significance of their contribution. Short papers will be evaluated on the quality of their focused contribution.
  • We prioritize new and ongoing research. Any overlap with prior publications must be disclosed and accompanied by substantive new contributions.
  • Include an AI-use statement following the ICLR 2027 author policy. Short/Tiny Papers must be primarily human-authored: AI assistance is permitted, but AI-generated papers are not allowed.

Accepted Papers and Non-Archival Policy

Accepted contributions will be presented as posters, with selected papers invited for contributed talks. Accepted papers will be publicly available on OpenReview by February 26, 2027. The workshop is non-archival and does not publish formal proceedings, so authors may subsequently submit their work to other venues.
